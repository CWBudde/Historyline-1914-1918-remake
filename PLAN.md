# PLAN — Historyline 1914-1918 remake

**Goal:** a complete, playable remake in Go/Ebitengine with newly authored art (2×, optional 4×), a fully
recreated UI and an optional original-rules profile.

**Art direction:** continuous painted illustration (see [art/README.md](art/README.md)). Keep the
original's gameplay IDs, map placement, connection topology and information layouts; never ship original art
or close reconstructions. Unit sprites and portraits follow their reference closely in composition at the
original size, in the painted style.
Terrain must remain reusable in different compatible tile arrangements. Match the reference's materials,
scale and lighting; do not achieve PULSE resemblance by substituting a painting of its exact lake outline.

**Rule sets:** `Classic` (the inherited Python behaviour), `Standard` (Classic with fixes), and the original
profile in `internal/dosrules` (not wired into the game yet). Keep them separate; never mix their mechanics.

Tick a box only with evidence you ran. Record decisions here, not their history.

## Where things stand

- **Rules engine** (`internal/game`): hex map, terrain and seasons, movement (Dijkstra, movement types),
  three-state fog with remembered units/owners, combat with counter fire and ammo, containers (HQ, depots,
  factories), capture, HQ victory, end turn. Unit stats and attacks follow the original unit table
  (`internal/data/testdata/unitdat.json`).
- **Front end** (`cmd/historyline`): loads the shipped levels, camera with zoom and independent UI scale and
  asset density, HiDPI and fullscreen, minimap, text info box for terrain and units, placeholder battle panel,
  hit/death/capture/round banners, reduced motion, keyboard/mouse controls, computer opponent.
- **Runtime art**: packs built by `cmd/hlasset` from `assets/index.json`; unit and terrain catalogs with
  context-aware composition (`internal/mapcompose`), drawn with `just play`. Craters and boulder
  fields use cluster stamps; knolls use overhanging standing art. Interface packs build, but the game does
  not use them yet.
- **AI** (`internal/ai`): greedy prototype through game commands and its own vision; plays all shipped levels.
- **Original rules** (`internal/dosrules`): combat (casualties, support, distance, experience, order handler),
  movement queries and routes, loading/unloading/exits, visibility and the phase change, with fixtures for the
  full game and the demo; the command cycle state `Game` (validation, queued attacks, resolution with ordered
  events, save/resume, victory and score, the energy account with repair and production).
- **Art**: masters and `prompt.md` in `art/<kind>/<family>/`, runtime PNGs in `assets/`, `art/index.json`
  generated by `just art-index`. 176 families (22 terrain with 179 runtime IDs, 48 units, 48 portraits,
  14 interface, 44 screens): 72 selected/revised families and 104 candidates. All 48 unit families have
  complete sprite and 1×/2×/4× portrait exports; all 75 gameplay unit IDs have matching family aliases.
  Review them in the art book (`just artbook`) and the comparison
  desk (`just gfx-game-review`), and the PULSE render against the reference with `just reference`.

#### The original's battle screen (`-rules dos`)

Decision (2026-10-22): under `-rules dos` the battle screen follows the original's layout. Two map panels
sit side by side, one per side, inside a riveted steel frame whose inner edge follows the outline of the
visible hex cells, with a status bar under each panel. Info, unit, contents, strategic-map and battle views
open inside a panel within the same frame. `Classic` and `Standard` keep the current single-map HUD.
The look is newly authored (painted steel, the remake's terrain and units); only layout, proportions,
information and behaviour follow the original.

Reference: local frames of the original at 320×200 under `original/reference/screenshots/` (git-ignored,
never committed): `dosbox/` (menus, every in-game view below, the phase change, both sides' turns on
PULSE) and `strategywiki/` (screens, action icons and their diagrams). `dosbox/game_pulse_start.png` is the
PULSE opening and matches StrategyWiki's Screen 3. Measurements are in those native pixels; the remake
scales them with its art (2×, optional 4×).

##### Anatomy of the reference

- **Screen** (312×200 used): left panel x 0–151, divider x 152–159, right panel x 160–311; map
  viewports y 0–179, status bars y 184–197.
- **Panels:** left = player A (the Central Powers on the shipped levels), right = player B. Each panel
  shows its side's own map (its units and what that side knows), its own cursor and its own status bar.
  Against the computer, the computer's panel stays visible and shows its view and its moves as they happen.
- **Viewport:** about 9 cell columns × 7½ cell rows (24×24-px cells, columns 16 px apart). The frame's
  inner edge is the outline of the outermost cells: teeth every 32 px along the top and bottom (12 px
  deep) and every 24 px along the sides (8 px deep). The view scrolls in whole cells, so the teeth always
  meet cell edges.
- **Frame:** olive-grey steel plates (neutral greys with an olive cast, about `#24241C`–`#B2B2A6`), a light
  bevel on edges facing the top-left light, a dark lip along the inner outline, round rivets along every
  tooth, the divider as a vertical post with a highlight line, a bolt at the foot of the divider between
  the status bars, scuffed highlights near the lower corners.
- **Status bar** (152×14 per panel): a recessed well with a near-black olive fill (`#181C00`) and a thin
  brown bevel (`#453828` / `#24180C`), one line of centred capitals in a pixel font. Text is pale green
  (`#BED392`) on the left and cream (`#FBE3C7`) on the right; numbers are amber.
  - Cursor on an own unit: group size at the left, unit name centred, the owner's flag at the right.
  - Otherwise a state message: waiting for the other side's mode; the computer carries out its orders;
    the unit has moved; confirm the mode change with F1 / leave with the fire button; the new mode; the
    computer analyses the position, with a thin progress bar along the bottom of the well.
- **Cursor:** a hex ring of the same steel around the cell. While fire is held, the ring becomes a solid
  hex plate that shows one action icon (cancel first). Pushing a direction while holding picks an action;
  releasing runs it. After an order the plain ring returns; the moved unit is drawn greyed (no hatched
  plate).
- **Action icons:** hex steel plates with an embossed glyph: cancel (X), move (four arrows), attack (fist),
  mode change (double arrow), open (box), strategic map (D on a scroll), info (?). Around the centre:
  move and attack above, mode change and open to the left, map to the right, info below. In a container:
  move (out) above, produce (hammer) to the left, cancel to the right, repair (wrench) below.
- **Move range:** every cell out of reach (terrain and units) is darkened to about 0.62 brightness;
  reachable cells keep full colour. Attack targets: check against further battle frames.
- **Unit info** (info on a unit): the map gives way to a steel inner plate with a sepia photo (top left,
  about 100×84), the unit's sprite on a small hex plate beside it, a brown name plate with brass rivets
  and a stats plate: weapon strength and range against air, ground and water; armour, speed, weight,
  maximum group size.
- **Game info** (info on the HQ): the map darkens and the text stands on it: round, level, mode, best
  and current score; a table with columns for side one, side two and the whole map (units, depots,
  factory units, energy total and income); move count and move limit.
- **Strategic map:** a dark steel plate with a recessed frame holding the whole map: terrain colour per
  cell, HQs as labelled plates in the side colours, depots as D plates, units as dots in the side colours,
  the panel's current view as a light rectangle.
- **Contents** (open on a container): a sepia photo of the unit under the cursor, a plate with the
  container's name and energy (stored, total), the unit's name plate, a large plate for details, and two
  rows of hex slots holding the units inside; the cursor moves over the slots.
- **Battle:** a sepia animation fills one panel inside the frame (air: close-ups of a pilot and a
  biplane) while the other panel shows the map. Which panel shows what: check against further battle
  frames. F9 turns the animations off.
- **Phase change:** both panels fade to greyscale with the confirmation prompts, go black with only the
  cursor plates left, show the new mode of each side on the grey maps, then colour returns while the
  computer's panel shows its analysis progress.

##### Remake design

- **Layout** (`layout.go`, pure maths, table-tested): two equal panels, the divider and the two status
  bars fill the window at any aspect ratio. The panel height sets the cell scale so that at least 7½ rows
  fit; extra width shows more columns (no letterboxing). The UI scale works as now. The smallest window
  keeps 7×6 cells per panel.
- **Frame** (drawn at runtime, not one painting): a tileable steel texture masked by the union of the
  panel's visible cells (`hexgrid` polygons). The bevel and lip come from the mask edge, the rivets sit at
  the tooth vertices; the divider post, bolt and status wells are fixed pieces. Cached per window size and
  zoom; cells beyond the map edge stay steel, so the frame closes in at the edge.
- **Camera per panel:** snaps to whole cells (column pairs, so the teeth keep their phase) with a short
  slide; follows its side's cursor and that side's revealed moves.
- **Panel modes** per side: map, unit info, game info, strategic map, contents, battle, phase change.
  Each panel draws its own mode; the other panel keeps running.
- **Input:** the human side's panel takes the mouse (click selects and confirms; hold and drag at least
  one cell picks a gesture; right-click opens a ring of every available icon around the cursor, a remake
  addition for mouse and touch) and key set A (arrows + space). A second human plays the right panel with
  key set B (D/C/X/V + Ctrl), both at the same time on one screen. This is the original's two-player mode
  and replaces hot-seat under `-rules dos`: each side acts in its own mode, and the phase changes once both
  confirm.
- **Computer panel:** shows the computer's view; its status bar shows the planner's progress and the
  orders it carries out. Whether a setting may close the computer's panel (shutter over the frame) is an
  open question.
- **Status bars** replace `dosStatusText` with per-panel messages from `dosControl` state and the unit
  line (group size, name, flag) in a pixel font, in the two panel colours.
- **Overlays** use the session's data: unit info from the `dosplay` unit view (weapon strength and range
  per target class, armour, speed, weight, group size), with the authored portraits through a sepia tint
  (no new portrait art); game info from the session (round, level, mode, score, units/depots/factory units
  per side, energy total and income, moves and limit); contents with repair and production (§3.1 step 6).
- **Battle:** sepia vignettes per domain (infantry, armour, artillery, air, naval) as short frame
  animations, with casualties in the status bar; the current battle panel is the fallback; F9 and reduced
  motion skip them.
- **Phase change:** greyscale shader on both panels, fade to black with the cursor plates, the new mode of
  each side, colour back; reduced motion shortens it.

##### Why repainting alone does not close the gap

1. **Ground ownership remains hex-based.** Standing summer crowns, bridges and knolls can overhang their
   cells, but winter forest still uses baked hex tiles. Seasonal layering and broader transition acceptance remain open.
2. **Transitions remain exported contexts.** The current 2× packs contain 110 ground, 111 forest and 159 hill
   contexts. Summer woodland has reusable floor/crown sources and irregular edge masks; a general material
   compositor and consistent transitions across all families remain open.
3. **Clusters and compounds use separate exporters.** Crater, boulder and knoll stamps cover PULSE, Civil
   and Mouse; seasonal HQ/depot/factory/massif paintings are already sliced across their cells. Sharing one
   mechanism and handling arbitrary new shapes remain architecture tasks, rather than missing paintings.
4. **Linear infrastructure uses fixed port tiles.** Curved painted roads/channels, standing bridges and path
   approaches exist. A general stroke renderer, broader meander variation and consistent mixed junctions
   remain open.
5. **Common grading remains unfinished.** Reference comparisons measure colour, contrast and edge detail,
   but consistent material, light and seasonal grading still need visual acceptance.
