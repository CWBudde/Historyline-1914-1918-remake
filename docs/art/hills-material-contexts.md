# Hills relief and context contract

The `-hills` builder uses a newly generated near-gray height control and the
independently authored meadow material. The height source is not a lit RGB
terrain texture: the builder computes slope and applies consistent upper-left
lighting. It never averages prelit hill paintings or mirrors whole hill tiles.
The exact source prompt and unchanged generated bytes are pinned by the
[generation recipe](../../art/recipes/hills/distinct-v1/height-01.json).

## World coordinates and contexts

The existing 96×80 polygon, six neighbor origins and complementary half-open
ownership remain unchanged. Valid cell origins satisfy `y=40*line` and
`x=142*column+71*(line mod 2)`, including negative lines. The source field repeats
after world translations `(568,0)` and `(0,320)`. Origins normalize to their
nonnegative phase within that rectangle; the lattice has 32 such phases.

Only summer `hills` and `grass` are supported. Six neighbor IDs are ordered
NE, SE, S, SW, NW, N. A profile descriptor has literal LF after every line:

```text
hills-v1
season=summer
center=<runtime ID>
phase=<normalized x>,<normalized y>
NE=<runtime ID>
SE=<runtime ID>
S=<runtime ID>
SW=<runtime ID>
NW=<runtime ID>
N=<runtime ID>
```

Its full lowercase SHA-256 identifies a rendering context, not a gameplay ID.
Grass normalizes its neighbors to grass and its phase to `(0,0)`, because the
owned grass tile stays byte-identical to the existing shared base. The default
`tiles/hills-2x.png` and `-4x.png` apply only at phase `(0,0)` with six grass
neighbors. A different origin or neighborhood requires its matching profile.
Different absolute line parities can show different terrain relief; parity
does not imply identical images.

The bounded bundle exports all 64 neighbor masks **at phase `(0,0)`**, all 32
isolated-hill phases, the normalized grass profile and the additional contexts used by its eight fixed
fixtures. It does not export all 64 masks at every phase, select runtime assets
or build arbitrary maps. Each fixture records nineteen actual sites, seven
visible cells, ordered neighbors, origins and context keys. Scene PNGs assemble
decoded exported tiles with ordinary alpha composition.

## Continuous relief and grass boundaries

Source coordinates vary continuously with absolute world position:

```text
u = (1-cos(2π*(x+113)/568))/2
v = (1-cos(2π*(y+79)/320))/2
H = 32 * bilinear Rec709 luminance(u,v) / 255
```

Central slope samples use a two-world-pixel half-step. This averages the slope
over a useful width rather than amplifying the source's 8-bit value steps.
The source is retained unchanged; its maximum RGB channel spread is four and
the loader accepts opaque square controls with channel spread at most eight.

For each actual grass site, the distance `d` to its filled polygon contributes
`smoothstep5(clamp((d-7)/12))`. The product attenuates the two slope components
**after** differentiation. Differentiating a height envelope instead created an
unwanted steep wall around clearings and was rejected. The final normal field
is a height-derived relief field with slope attenuation; it need not integrate
to a single globally gated height surface.

The normal is `normalize(-gx,-gy,1)`, the light is
`normalize(-0.55,-0.4,0.75)`, and illumination uses 0.35 ambient plus 0.65 diffuse,
normalized so flat grass has factor one. The factor clamps to `[0.55,1.25]`.
The existing shared grass sampler supplies material pixels, with exact flat
returns and half-up rounding after nonflat RGB multiplication.

An isolated hill with six grass neighbors also receives a small smooth
elliptical height cue before slope sampling. The ellipse has semiaxes 28 and 22
world pixels and a compact cubic falloff. Its height is at most six, fading out
where the existing global relief is strong; nine interior lighting samples
determine that fade. The 32 phases give the ellipse different orientations,
while world lighting stays fixed. This restores a readable low knoll in phases
that otherwise looked like ordinary grass. Connected hills do not receive this
cue, so it introduces no repeating mound stamps in continuous hill regions.

The cue's support, slope half-step and diagonal reduction reach sum to less
than the minimum forty-world-pixel center-to-edge distance:
`28+2+1.944544<40`. It cannot change a tile boundary or another cell's owned
filter footprint. Its exact amplitude, angle and sampled global lighting RMS
are recorded per exported profile.

## Filtering, ownership and local reach

Render an opaque 416×352 4× overscan, then apply whole unmasked Lanczos3 to
208×176 at 2×. Crop four world pixels and apply complementary half-open polygon
ownership separately at each density. Premasking before filtering would make
dark or transparent seams.

The nearest omitted grass polygon is 46 world pixels from the owned polygon.
On the polygon expanded by four pixels it remains at least 42 away, beyond the
19-pixel attenuation support. Thus the center and six neighbors suffice for
the contextual gate. Global height/slope sampling does not depend on omitted
hill sites. No locality claim is made for unused corners of the overscan
rectangle outside the owned filter footprint.

The actual 4×→2× filter taps reach at most 1.375 world pixels per axis, or
1.944544 diagonally. A final pixel within four world pixels of a grass boundary
therefore samples only zero-gate material: `4+1.944544<7`. The final quiet band
matches independently filtered grass exactly. Slopes are sampled before the
context gate, so the slope half-step does not enlarge that gate's reach.

## Remaining acceptance

These are provisional offline candidates. Other-ground transitions, imported
maps, variation over repeated periods, fractional zoom, unit readability,
runtime selection and release acceptance remain open. Completing the enabled
terrain bindings does not complete native-resource, unit or UI coverage.
